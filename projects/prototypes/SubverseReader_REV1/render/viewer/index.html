SubverseReader

An e-paper reader the size of a backstage pass. The phone app pulls zines, ebooks and PDFs from Subverse and sends them over; then the phone can stay off. Radios sleep while you read, so a charge lasts weeks. No touch screen, just keys. Every unit has USB-C. Target: $50 or less, buildable on a home bench, open hardware.

Tap to collect

SR-14 reads NFC tags. Stick them on posters, flyers, record sleeves, the merch table. Tap the back of the pass on one and the zine behind it lands on your shelf. A tag carries one of three things:

On the tagTagWhat the pass does
A link to the zine on Subverse (NDEF URI)NTAG213 and up, ~40 bytesQueues it. The Wi-Fi build fetches a pack rendered for its panel; the tethered build hands the link to the phone over BLE, and the phone downloads, renders and sends the pages back.
A micro-zine stored in the tagNTAG216 (888 B): a poem, a setlist, a manifesto; ST25DV64K (8 KB): several pages of text or one small 1-bit imageShows it at once, no network. Compressed plain text, laid out by the pass's own font.
BothNTAG216 and upShows the first page now, fetches the full issue later.
Two builds, one enclosure. Wi-Fi (ESP32-C5) fetches by itself on any band, including a phone hotspot, and falls back to the phone when there is no Wi-Fi. Tethered (nRF52840, no Wi-Fi) always goes through the phone app, has the lowest sleep current, and its USB-C is a USB drive.
The pass is a tag too. A dynamic tag (ST25DV on the Wi-Fi build, the nRF52840's own NFC peripheral on the tethered one) lets a phone tap the pass to get the link to what you're reading, or to pair over BLE without a menu. Two passes can trade zines the same way.

The Wi-Fi fetch needs packs rendered server-side for each panel profile (Subverse, or an open pack-render service), since the pass can't render PDFs. Links are followed only for Subverse or domains the user approves. The reader field is on only for 10 s after TAP.

Start building

Pick a pass and how many you're building. The orders are listed slowest first, so the long waits start first. Prices are from the Nov 6–7 snapshot; shipping is one standard fee per store, with no tax or tariffs.

Start with SR-1, SR-1C or SR-8. They're the only passes you can build today with no board design, and parts arrive in about 10 days. SR-1 and SR-8 use Seeed's EE05 board, which already has the panel connector, three keys and a battery socket with a switch; you add a XIAO (ESP32-S3 Plus or nRF52840 Plus), a Good Display panel and a cell. The XIAO solders flat by the pads underneath it, so you need hot air or a reflow oven. SR-1C keeps 5 GHz Wi-Fi: a DFRobot FireBeetle 2 ESP32-C5, a 24-pin panel adapter and three hand-wired keys.
Not drawn yet: the custom board for SR-2, SR-4 to SR-7, SR-9 to SR-12 and SR-14, and the printable enclosure for every pass. You can still order the slow parts now: the ESP32-C5 modules take about four weeks. SR-3 and SR-13 wait for the sold-out M5 kit (or a LilyGo T5; SR-3 has a build guide in the repo, docs/Gray-Pass-SR3-build-guide.md), and SR-4 for its panel and power chip. SR-1C's panel adapter (Adafruit 4224) is a placeholder: its price and fit with the 4.2in panel aren't checked.

    Radios, storage, loading

    Why the ESP32-C5. People don't know whether their router or hotspot is 2.4 or 5 GHz, and an S3 simply can't see a 5 GHz-only network. The C5 does both bands, and the app never asks: the pass hosts its own network (5 GHz channels 36–48 first, 2.4 GHz if that's quieter), the app joins it silently, and BLE is the fallback if Wi-Fi fails.
    The catch. The C5's USB port is serial and JTAG only, with no USB OTG, so it can't appear as a USB drive. On C5 passes the microSD card is the mass loader (pull it, copy packs, put it back), plus a Web Serial page that streams packs over the cable. No ESP32 has both 5 GHz and USB OTG.

    How the software works

    The rule from maplet holds: the phone renders, the pass shows. All the hard work (PDF rasterising, EPUB layout, fonts, dithering) happens on the phone at the exact resolution of the pass. The pass stores finished pages and draws them. Every variant runs the same reader core; SR-8 runs it on Zephyr behind a thin platform layer.

    The app

    Built on Switchback's Flutter code (BLE transport, pairing, Android first) but as its own app, since the audience is different.

    Subverse. The catalog: issues, new this week, search.
    Library. Downloads on the phone, plus PDFs and EPUBs shared in from other apps.
    Pass. The shelf on the pass, free space, battery, last sync. Changes queue until the next sync.
    Preview. The exact pages the pass will show, so layout choices are made by looking.
    Badge. The sleep image: a cover, a dithered photo, or a pass with your name and role.

    Battery: auto-off, any key wakes

    A still e-paper page costs nothing, so the pass can be off nearly all the time and still show what you were reading. The custom boards have no power switch: every key is the on button. After each page the pass drops through a ladder of sleep levels by itself.

    LevelWhenDrawOn screenWake
    Awakea key was pressed25–40 mA (ESP32) · 3–5 mA (nRF)page being drawn—
    Linger10 s after the last key~0.35 mAthe pageany key, instant
    Asleepafter linger~16 µA ESP32 · ~8 µA nRFthe page, or the badge (a setting)any key, ~0.3 s
    Off7 idle days, or under 5 %~3 µA (cell protection only)badge, or a "charge me" cardany key: cold boot, reopens the saved page
    How every key turns it on. On the custom ESP32 boards each key has a BAT54C diode pair. One diode pulls a P-MOSFET latch that switches the regulator on from off; the other pulls a shared any-key line that wakes the chip from deep sleep. The firmware holds the latch while it works and lets go to switch off. Plugging in USB powers it on too. This adds about $1 at 5 units and $0.30 at 100. nRF52840 passes skip the latch, because their System OFF state is already under 1 µA and any pin wakes them. The thumbstick and thumbwheel don't wake a pass; their keys do.
    Separate timeouts. Sync 3 min, front light 2 min (dims at 1), NFC field 10 s or the first read. Position is saved on every page turn, so going fully off loses nothing. The pass stops at a safe 3.4 V instead of letting the cell's protection cut in at 2.5 V, which ages the cell.

    Read these as estimates. They include a 1.5× margin on every active figure. With deep sleep done right, page turns and the light set battery life, and standby lasts a year or more, limited by LiPo self-discharge. The weak spot is sleep current: an unswitched microSD card or a module board's LED at 250 µA cuts every figure to 2–5 months. Panel and card sit on a switched rail for this reason, and it is the first thing to measure on the bench. Not modelled: about 20 % less capacity near 0 °C.

    Enclosure

    • Two-part PETG shell, four M2 screws into heat-set inserts, 1.6 mm walls.
    • Panel sits 1.3 mm behind the bezel so the glass is protected on a lanyard.
    • 18 mm lanyard slot and a 25 mm split ring; USB-C on the bottom edge; microSD on the left edge.
    • A printed vinyl band above the screen carries the pass graphic and can be swapped.
    Back pockets. SR-2 and SR-4: an ID-1 card plus a folded ticket (inner 88.6 × 56 × 1.6 mm), thumb notch, window in the back wall. SR-7: the same idea sized for an A7 paper mini-zine (74 × 105 mm). Each adds about 4 mm of depth.

    Open questions

    1. Subverse site: which site is it, and can it publish a catalog feed (OPDS 2.0 or a JSON index)?
    2. Flagship chip: stay on the S3 (USB drive, 2.4 GHz only) or move to the C5 (dual-band, loads by microSD or Web Serial)?
    3. App: a new app (io.iotone.subversereader?) or a Subverse mode inside Switchback?
    4. Reading size: 5.83in for the flagship, or 4.2in with column mode?
    5. SR-14 Wi-Fi fetch: can Subverse serve packs pre-rendered per panel, or do we run an open pack-render service?
    6. SR-9 stick: keep the $3.50 Adafruit 444 (no click), or a pressable stick (Adafruit 512) so OK sits under the thumb?
    7. 16 greys or buyable? The 16-grey passes (SR-3, SR-4, SR-13) depend on parts that come and go. Commit to LilyGo boards when they are in stock, or make SR-12's 3.97in 800×480 the sharp-text pass?
    8. Front light on the 5.83in? Good Display offers it as an add-on; no price found yet.
