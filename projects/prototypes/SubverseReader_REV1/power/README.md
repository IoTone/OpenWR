# SubverseReader REV1 battery life

Generated by `battery_model.py`; edit that, not this file. **Estimates**, to be replaced by bench numbers (PPK2 or Joulescope on the battery lead). Usable capacity is 85% of rated; LiPo self-discharge 2 %/month is included in standby.

**Profiles.** *Reader*: 300 page turns a day (2–3 h of reading), nothing else. *Festival*: 150 page turns, one sync a day, plus the variant's extra (SR-6 one hour of front light; SR-14 ten taps and two fetches). *Asleep*: never touched, deep sleep with the page on screen. *Off*: after 7 idle days the latch cuts power; only the cell's protection IC draws.

## What auto-off protects against

Time to flat if the pass were left in one awake state with no timeout (1200 mAh cell, 85 % usable). Every one of these states has its own timeout in firmware.

| Left on | ESP32 passes | nRF52840 passes |
|---|---|---|
| Menu left open, CPU in light sleep | 4 mo | 1 yr+ |
| Menu left open, CPU awake (no sleep at all) | 36 h | 11 d |
| Sync left running (Wi-Fi / BLE connected) | 11 h | 7 d |
| SR-6 front light left on (mid) | 40 h | — |
| SR-14 NFC field left on | 20 h | 20 h |

## Assumptions

- Every active figure (page turns, sync, light, NFC) is multiplied by 1.5 for real-world firmware; the per-component figures are datasheet-typical.
- Page turn = wake + read and decompress + push frame + refresh + 10 s light sleep before deep sleep. 1-bit panels do a full refresh every 20 pages.
- Parallel 16-grey panels (SR-3, SR-4, SR-13) keep the S3 awake for the whole waveform (~1–1.5 s at ~90 mA). A fast 2-level text mode would cut this roughly in half.
- Deep sleep: ESP32-C5 ~12 µA (datasheet typ), S3 ~9 µA, nRF52840 ~2 µA (chip, RTC on), plus board overhead: 6 µA on our boards (2 µA LDO, protection IC, charger); module boards are guesses until measured.
- Panel and microSD sit on a switched rail. An unswitched microSD idles at 100–250 µA and would be the largest sleep draw on the pass.
- Not modelled: cold-temperature capacity loss (~20 % at 0 °C), cell ageing past 300 cycles.
