Status proposal and clickable prototype, 2026-11-03 Runs on Snap Spectacles ('24), Lens Studio, TypeScript Cameras Sony α7R III via AlphaFriend, plus ESP32 cameras over BLE

Imaging Edge Mobile, moved from your hand to your eyes. The α7R III sits on the tripod; you walk to the edge of the frame, raise the glasses, and set the shot: f-stop, shutter speed, ISO and EV on dials you pinch; a low-res peek through the viewfinder every few seconds; shoot, and the postview drops into a contact sheet. A small ESP32 camera can stand anywhere else as a second angle, and the glasses run them all.

1. How it connects

A Bluetooth lens gets no internet and no raw sockets, and a lens can't bond. The α7R III speaks Wi-Fi. So a StickC Plus SE, AlphaFriend, sits between them: Bluetooth to the glasses, Wi-Fi to the camera's own access point (Ctrl w/ Smartphone), where it drives Sony's JSON-RPC API and shrinks liveview frames before they cross the slow Bluetooth hop. ESP32 cameras need no friend: they are Bluetooth devices themselves.

Why not straight to the camera? A lens can hold Bluetooth or internet, not both, unless the glasses run Extended Permissions, and then the lens can't be published. A lens also has no plain TCP or HTTP to a LAN address. The direct path stays an experiment on our developer glasses.

2. The prototype

The lens below works. Switch views along the top, change camera on the Rack (the fourth card is the glasses' own camera), turn the dials, shoot, then page back through each camera's reel. The viewfinder peek is real 80×53, one bit per pixel, which is what fits through Bluetooth in under a second; the exposure meter and the peek both react to your settings against a golden-hour scene at EV 12.

Photo streaming

Pictures leave the camera at three sizes, and each size has its own path. The camera's liveview is a stream of 640-wide JPEGs at up to 30 fps, and its postview is a 2 MP JPEG. Bluetooth to the glasses carries about 1.4 KB/s. So everything that reaches the glasses is made on AlphaFriend, small enough to fit through.

Postview80×53 right after the shot; 240×160 at 2 bits on request, in four interlaced passesfirst pass in 0.2 s, sharp in about 9 sAfter actTakePicture the camera returns a postview URL. AlphaFriend fetches it, scales it down and keeps it until the next shot, so the glasses can ask again.
Full resolutionnothing—A 42 MP JPEG is about 15 MB: three hours over Bluetooth. It stays on the camera's card. At the end of the session AlphaFriend lets go of the camera's AP, and the camera's own Send to Smartphone moves the keepers. The Sheet marks which ones to keep.
  • Only one camera streams. The link is shared, so the active camera streams and the others stay quiet. Switching cameras on the Rack moves the stream.
  • Shooting comes first. A command line (shoot, f!) goes ahead of the next stream frame, so streaming never delays a shot by more than one row.
  • The Scout streams the same way at 96×72 and 2 bits. Its own camera and PSRAM do the shrinking, and it doesn't need Wi-Fi.
  • A faster path is an experiment. On our Extended Permissions glasses, AlphaFriend could run its own Wi-Fi AP next to the camera link and serve MJPEG. That only works if a lens can reach a LAN address, and the lens could never be published. It's a probe for later.

Specs as a camera

The glasses have a camera too, and SwitchbackXR already shoots with it (snap/Viewfinder.ts, snap/Dither.ts): a head-locked target, the camera frame cropped to it, dithered on the lens. LightScopeXR puts that on the Rack as a fourth camera called Specs. It needs no link, so its peek runs at the lens's own frame rate.

  • Stylized on purpose. Like the maplet, a Specs frame is the picture: 1-bit grain (Floyd–Steinberg), 1-bit screen (Bayer) or 2-bit, cropped to the maplet's 212×104, 3:2 or square. Nothing is a stand-in for a full-resolution original.
  • Stored on an SD card. The lens keeps the last few; each shot is also written to the card of a linked Scout or maplet with roll-put. A 212×104 1-bit frame is 2.7 KB, about 2.7 s over Bluetooth, in the background. A card holds millions of them.
  • The lens needs a camera permission. As in SwitchbackXR, declaring InternetModule lets the camera and Bluetooth run together in a Transparent Permission lens, which can still be published.
  • It joins Sync. Fire all can include the glasses, so the α7R III, the Scout and your own point of view take the same moment.

3. N cameras

Every camera is a link in Links.ts with a driver and a list of capabilities (Specs is a local driver with no link). The view draws what the camera reports, so the α7R III gets four exposure dials, the Scout gets resolution, quality and flash, and the maplet gets a shutter and nothing else. Adding a camera type means a new driver, not a new view.

interface CameraLink {
  id: string; name: string; tint: GlassTint;          // Fog, Glacier, Headlamp, ...
  via: "alphafriend" | "direct";                      // proxy or the camera itself
  caps: { dials: DialSpec[]; af: boolean; rec: boolean;
          lv: { w: number; h: number; bits: 1 | 2 } | null; last: boolean };
  state: { link: "scan"|"up"|"lost"; rssi: number; battery?: number; left?: string };
  send(line: string): Promise<string>;             // one NUS line, one reply
}
  • Link budget. The glasses held three Bluetooth links at once in SwitchbackXR. Plan for three cameras, test four. Only the active camera peeks; the others answer cam? every 5 s.
  • One AlphaFriend per Sony. The camera's access point takes one client, so a second Sony means a second StickC. The contract is the same, so the lens does not care.
  • Sync fire. The glasses write shoot to each link in turn: about one Bluetooth write apart, 40–100 ms. For tighter sync, a later arm/fire pair: each device arms, then a single short write fires all of them.
  • Scout hardware. AI-Thinker ESP32-CAM (OV2640, 4 MB PSRAM, microSD) or the XIAO S3 Sense we already use for the maplet. With PSRAM it can afford a 96×72 two-bit peek.

4. AlphaFriend at setup time

The StickC's 135×240 screen answers the questions you have when it isn't working yet: did it find the camera's network, does the API answer, are the glasses connected, what was the last thing asked. The big button re-scans; the side button cycles saved cameras.

5. The contract

Plain lines over the Nordic UART service, one reply each, the same shape as goggl1 and the maplet. The Scout implements the subset its caps list.

6. AlphaFriend firmware: Alpha Fairy on ESPIDFORTH

Alpha Fairy is written for Arduino (arduino-esp32, which itself runs on ESP-IDF); there is no ESP-IDF fork of it that we could find. So we fork it for its camera knowledge and port that knowledge onto the stack goggl1 and the maplet already run: ESPIDFORTH on ESP-IDF 5.3, NimBLE NUS console, bundles and OTA. ESPIDFORTH already builds for the plain ESP32 (env:esp32), which is what the Plus SE's PICO-D4 is.

  • C for the wire, Forth for the policy. HTTP, JSON, JPEG decode and dithering are C words, the same split as the maplet's cam_words.c. Retries, sequences, what to push and when are Forth, so they change by bundle without a reflash.
  • No PSRAM. One liveview JPEG buffer (~40 KB), decoded straight to 80×53 with the TJpgDec that is already in the ESP32's ROM. No frame is ever held whole in RAM.
  • The screen. ST7789 135×240 on esp_lcd; the three states in section 4.
  • Keep the license. Alpha Fairy is MIT: the fork keeps its notice, and the ported files say where they came from.

8. Open questions

  1. Which API methods does this α7R III list? Some bodies return 40404 for exposure calls. L0 answers it.
  2. Wi-Fi and Bluetooth on one PICO radio. Liveview over Wi-Fi while serving NUS: measure the round trip when both are busy.
  3. BT remote while in Ctrl w/ Smartphone? If the camera allows both, AlphaFriend gets a shutter that survives a Wi-Fi drop.
  4. PC Remote over Wi-Fi on the α7R III? If it works, port PtpIpCamera too: PTP/IP gives more properties and faster liveview than JSON-RPC.
  5. Can AlphaFriend list the card? The JSON-RPC avContent service (getContentList) needs Contents Transfer mode, which may not coexist with remote shooting. Without it, the α7R III reel holds only the shots AlphaFriend saw.
